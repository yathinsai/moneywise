# Teen Finance & Business Simulator

## Interactive VanillaJS Financial Literacy Experience

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this app is to empower teens ages **12--19** with
practical financial literacy and business skills through engaging,
interactive experiences.

Rather than teaching finance only through definitions and textbook
lessons, the app places teens inside realistic decisions involving
money, saving, investing, inflation, careers, and small businesses.

The goal is to help teens:

-   Develop a strong financial mindset.
-   Practice making informed money decisions.
-   Understand the consequences of financial choices.
-   Connect classroom concepts to real-world economic events.
-   Explore basic business and entrepreneurship principles.
-   Build confidence toward financial independence.

The experience should feel less like a traditional course and more like
**chatting with a financial coach while playing through real-life
scenarios**.

## 2. Core Learning Pillars

### Financial Literacy

Introduce practical concepts such as:

-   Budgeting
-   Saving
-   Emergency funds
-   Investing
-   Compound growth
-   Inflation
-   Stock market fundamentals
-   Needs vs. wants
-   Income and expenses
-   Risk and reward
-   Responsible money management

### Business & Entrepreneurship

Help teens understand foundational business concepts through relatable
examples:

-   Revenue
-   Expenses
-   Profit
-   Pricing
-   Marketing
-   Customer demand
-   Reinvestment
-   Business growth
-   Competition
-   Cost increases
-   Adapting a business to changing conditions

### Real-World Connections

Bridge financial education with events teens hear about in everyday
life.

Examples include:

-   Inflation
-   Housing costs
-   Technology shifts
-   AI and changing careers
-   Layoffs and job-market changes
-   Interest rates
-   Supply-chain disruptions
-   Energy-price changes
-   Market volatility

The app should explain these events neutrally and show how they can
affect personal finances or business decisions.

### Financial Independence

Teach teens how everyday financial decisions contribute to long-term
independence.

Key behaviors include:

-   Creating a budget
-   Setting savings goals
-   Avoiding unnecessary spending
-   Building an emergency fund
-   Investing for long-term goals
-   Understanding opportunity cost
-   Developing multiple skills and income opportunities

## 3. Experience Model

The application uses a **conversational bot simulator** rather than a
traditional static quiz.

The bot acts like a friendly financial coach.

Typical interaction:

1.  The bot introduces a situation.
2.  The teen chooses between multiple actions.
3.  The app evaluates the decision.
4.  The bot provides immediate feedback.
5.  The scenario branches or continues.
6.  The teen sees the financial consequence.
7.  The app explains the lesson.

Example:

> **Coach:** You earned \$50 this weekend. Your friends want to see a
> movie, but you are also saving for a new phone. What would you do?

The learner then chooses an option and sees how that choice affects
spending, savings, or progress toward a goal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Scenario-Based Learning

Scenarios should use situations teens can understand or may encounter.

Examples:

-   Saving for sneakers or a phone
-   Managing allowance or part-time-job income
-   Planning a weekend with friends
-   Running a lemonade stand
-   Selling bracelets online
-   Organizing a school event
-   Starting a small social-media business
-   Investing imaginary money
-   Comparing saving versus investing
-   Responding when prices increase

Each scenario should include:

-   A relatable situation
-   2--4 choices
-   Immediate feedback
-   Financial consequences
-   A short explanation
-   Optional branching decisions
-   A final lesson or takeaway

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Sample Budgeting Scenario

**Coach:**

> You have \$50 for a weekend hangout. You want snacks, movie tickets,
> and you also want to save some money. What should you do first?

**Choices**

-   A. Spend most of it on snacks.
-   B. Make a list and estimate the costs.
-   C. Buy the movie tickets immediately.

If the learner selects **B**:

> Great choice. Planning your expenses first helps you understand how
> much you can spend without losing sight of your savings goal.

The bot can then continue:

> After estimating your costs, you realize the evening will cost about
> \$35. What should you do with the remaining \$15?

This creates a conversation instead of a single isolated quiz question.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Sample Savings Scenario

**Coach:**

> You want to buy something that costs \$200. You earn \$20 each week
> and want to reach your goal in 10 weeks. How much would you need to
> save each week?

**Choices**

-   A. \$10
-   B. \$20
-   C. \$5

If the learner chooses **B**:

> Correct. \$20 × 10 weeks = \$200. But saving every dollar also means
> you have nothing available for other expenses. A real budget may
> require either more time or additional income.

This teaches both the math and the trade-off behind the decision.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Sample Business Growth Scenario

**Coach:**

> You started a small online bracelet shop, but sales are slow. What
> could you try first?

**Choices**

-   A. Immediately lower every price.
-   B. Show the products to more potential customers through social
    media.
-   C. Stop selling.

If the learner selects **B**:

> Good thinking. Before changing your price, first determine whether
> enough people know your product exists.

Follow-up:

> More people are visiting your page, but few are buying. What should
> you investigate next?

Possible choices could explore pricing, product quality, customer needs,
or competition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Profit and Reinvestment Scenario

**Coach:**

> Your small business earned \$100 in profit this month. What could you
> do with the money?

Possible choices:

-   Spend all of it.
-   Reinvest some into the business.
-   Save some for unexpected costs.
-   Split it between reinvestment and savings.

Instead of always having one simplistic "correct" answer, advanced
scenarios should teach that **multiple choices can be reasonable
depending on the goal and circumstances**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Inflation & Real-World Scenarios

## 9. Understanding Inflation

Inflation should be introduced through changes teens can easily
visualize.

**Coach:**

> Last year, the sneakers you wanted cost \$100. This year, the same
> pair costs \$110. Your \$100 still exists, but it can no longer buy
> the same product.

The app explains:

> That loss of purchasing power is one effect of inflation.

The learner then decides how to respond:

-   Save longer.
-   Find an alternative product.
-   Increase income.
-   Adjust another part of the budget.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Housing Cost Scenario

**Coach:**

> Imagine apartment rents are increasing faster than your expected
> income. You are planning your first independent budget. What could you
> consider?

Choices could include:

-   Saving more before moving.
-   Sharing housing costs with roommates.
-   Considering a less expensive location.
-   Reducing other expenses.

**Lesson:**

Housing costs can change how much income is available for
transportation, food, savings, entertainment, and investing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Job Market Scenario

**Coach:**

> Imagine companies in your area are hiring fewer workers while everyday
> prices continue increasing. What could someone do to make their
> finances more resilient?

Possible choices:

-   Reduce non-essential expenses.
-   Build emergency savings.
-   Develop additional skills.
-   Look for additional income opportunities.

**Lesson:**

Inflation becomes especially challenging when income does not rise as
quickly as expenses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. AI and Career Change Scenario

**Coach:**

> AI changes the way some jobs are performed. Some roles shrink, while
> new roles and businesses appear. Someone wants to learn new skills to
> stay competitive. What financial factors should they consider?

Possible considerations:

-   Training costs
-   Time required for learning
-   Current living expenses
-   Potential future income
-   Opportunity cost

**Lesson:**

Technology can create both disruption and opportunity. Financial
planning can make career transitions easier to manage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Global Events and Price Changes

Use current events carefully and neutrally rather than presenting
temporary headlines as permanent facts.

Example:

**Coach:**

> Imagine a geopolitical conflict disrupts global oil supplies. Fuel and
> shipping costs increase. Your small business ships physical products
> to customers. What could you do?

Possible choices:

-   Review product pricing.
-   Compare delivery providers.
-   Reduce packaging or shipping costs.
-   Source products closer to customers.
-   Explore digital products where appropriate.

**Lesson:**

Events happening far away can affect transportation, production, prices,
household budgets, and business decisions.

# Interaction Design

## 14. Branching Choices

Choices should influence what happens next.

Example:

**Decision**

Spend \$40 of a \$50 budget.

**Consequence**

Savings decrease to \$10.

**Next decision**

A \$15 unexpected expense appears.

The learner now has to decide whether to:

-   Skip another purchase.
-   Use existing savings.
-   Find additional income.
-   Borrow money.

This creates a simple financial simulation rather than a collection of
disconnected questions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Instant Feedback

Feedback should explain **why** a decision matters.

Suggested feedback types:

**Strong Decision**\
The choice supports both the immediate goal and long-term financial
health.

**Reasonable Trade-Off**\
The choice can work, but the learner should understand what they are
giving up.

**Risky Decision**\
The choice may create future financial pressure.

**Try Again**\
Provide a hint without immediately revealing the answer.

Color-coded feedback can make these outcomes easy to recognize, while
text and icons should also communicate meaning for accessibility.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Tone and Language

The application should feel:

-   Friendly
-   Encouraging
-   Practical
-   Age appropriate
-   Non-judgmental
-   Clear
-   Occasionally playful

The bot should celebrate thoughtful decision-making rather than simply
rewarding memorization.

Avoid:

-   Lecturing
-   Complicated financial jargon
-   Shaming poor choices
-   Treating every scenario as having only one correct answer
-   Political framing of economic events

# VanillaJS Application Direction

## 17. Technical Philosophy

The first version should remain lightweight.

Recommended MVP stack:

-   HTML5
-   CSS / CSS Custom Properties
-   Vanilla JavaScript
-   JSON-based question and scenario data
-   `localStorage` for local progress
-   No login required
-   No backend required for the initial MVP
-   Static hosting such as GitHub Pages

This keeps the project inexpensive, easy to maintain, and simple for a
student team to understand.

# Product Vision

The long-term goal is not simply to build a financial quiz.

The goal is to create a **teen financial decision simulator** where
users can experiment with money choices safely and see how those
decisions affect their future.

A learner should gradually move from:

**Learn → Decide → See Consequences → Reflect → Try Again**

Future versions could expand into:

-   Virtual monthly budgets
-   Simulated jobs and income
-   Emergency expenses
-   Credit and debt
-   College-cost decisions
-   Investing simulations
-   Small-business simulations
-   Economic-event simulations
-   Financial goals
-   Classroom or workshop modes

The central design principle is:

> **Don't just tell teens how money works. Let them make decisions and
> experience what happens next.**
